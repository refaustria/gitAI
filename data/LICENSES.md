# Dataset licences and provenance

Every corpus used by this project, with its licence and terms. Recorded **before
downloading**, because some corpora forbid redistribution — which is a second
reason, independent of size, that corpus content never enters git.

Machine-readable provenance (URL, SHA-256, size, retrieval date) lives in
`raw/MANIFEST.json`, which *is* committed. The corpora it describes are not.

---

## TinyShakespeare

- **Licence:** Public domain (the works of Shakespeare); the compilation is
  distributed under MIT as part of `karpathy/char-rnn`.
- **Source:** https://github.com/karpathy/char-rnn
- **Use here:** Stage 0 smoke test. ~1 MB, trains in minutes, used to verify the
  pipeline and to seed CI-scale checks.
- **Redistribution:** unrestricted.

## TinyStories

- **Licence:** CDLA-Sharing-1.0. **Check the current terms on the dataset page
  before redistributing** — the registry entry records what was true at the time
  of writing, not a guarantee about today.
- **Source:** https://huggingface.co/datasets/roneneldan/TinyStories
- **Citation:** Eldan & Li, *TinyStories: How Small Can Language Models Be and
  Still Speak Coherent English?* (2023), arXiv:2305.07759
- **Use here:** the primary corpus. Synthetic stories in a 3–4 year-old's
  vocabulary; models of 1–35M parameters produce coherent English on it, which is
  what makes a no-GPU from-scratch LLM a real project rather than a toy.
- **Note:** synthetic, generated by GPT-3.5/GPT-4. Relevant to the research
  question — a corpus that is *itself* model output is an unusual starting point
  for a study of what happens when models train on model output, and any
  collapse result should say so explicitly rather than quietly inherit it.

## Adding a corpus

1. Add a `Source` to `REGISTRY` in `src/gitai/data/acquire.py`, with its licence.
2. Add a section here before downloading anything.
3. Leave `sha256=None` if you do not have a trustworthy checksum. It will be
   recorded on first download and enforced from then on. **Never invent one** —
   a wrong checksum in a registry fails against correct data and teaches you to
   skip the check.
